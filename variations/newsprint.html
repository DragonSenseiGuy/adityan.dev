Student Dev Ships Odd Little Apps — And Won't Stop

By Aditya N · a.k.a. "Dragon"

I'm a student developer at Hack Club, based in Boston, building AI tools, Slack bots, and odd little apps. Most of them start as a small itch and end up shipped — which, as any reader knows, is the hard part.

From automated code reviewers to self-hostable RSS readers, the work spans Python, Vue, and whatever a YSWS program throws my way next. The through-line: make something small and useful, then actually put it in the world.

Opinion — A Little Context

I go by "Dragon" around the internet. Most of what I make starts as a small itch — a bot that would make a community more fun, a tool that would save me ten minutes a day — and turns into a shipped project.

I spend a lot of time in the Hack Club community, building things alongside other teenage makers: Slack bots, AI plugins, game jams, and whatever a YSWS program throws at me next. The company is good and the deadlines are self-imposed, which suits me.

The habit is simple: notice the itch, build the small thing, ship it, repeat. It compounds faster than you'd expect.
